\subsection{Transistor Networks}
\label{sec:pre_def}
Let $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, \ldots, x_N)$ be $N$ Boolean input variables and
$\pi \in \{0,1\}^N$ an input pattern.
The \emph{literal alphabet} is
$\mathcal{L} = \{x_1, \lnot x_1, \ldots, x_N, \lnot x_N\}$, and a literal
$\ell$ evaluates to $\ell(\pi) \in \{0,1\}$ under $\pi$.

\begin{definition}[Transistor Network]
\label{def:tnet}
A \emph{transistor network} is an undirected multigraph $G = (V, E)$ with two
distinguished terminals $\SRC \neq \SNK \in V$ and edges
$E \subseteq \{(u, v, \ell) : u \neq v \in V,\ \ell \in \mathcal{L}\}$, each
edge being a transistor controlled by the literal $\ell$.
Under an input pattern $\pi$ a transistor is \emph{closed} iff
$\ell(\pi) = 1$, and $G$ \emph{conducts} under $\pi$ iff some
$\SRC$--$\SNK$ path uses only closed transistors.
Edges are unordered, so $(u,v,\ell)$ and $(v,u,\ell)$ denote the same
transistor; the orientation in our action space (Section~\ref{sec:mdp}) is an
artifact of the generation order, not of the network.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Correctness.}
For a Boolean function $g : \{0,1\}^N \to \{0,1\}$, let $\Pon = g^{-1}(1)$ and
$\Poff = g^{-1}(0)$ denote its on- and off-patterns.
A transistor network $G$ \emph{covers} $g$ if it conducts under every
$\pi \in \Pon$ (\emph{coverage}), and is \emph{safe} for $g$ if it conducts
under no $\pi \in \Poff$ (\emph{safety}); it \emph{correctly implements} $g$
iff both hold.
We name the two conditions separately because safety applies to \emph{partial}
networks as well: every intermediate state of our generative process is safe,
whereas coverage is attained only at termination.

\subsection{Two Synthesis Problems}
\label{sec:pre_prob}

\paragraph{Problem A: network synthesis.}
Given a switching function $g$, find a smallest transistor network that
correctly implements it,
\[
  \argmin\ \{\, |E(G)| \;:\; G \text{ correctly implements } g \,\}.
\]
The feasible set is non-empty (Theorem~\ref{thm:sop}), so the minimum is well
defined.  This is the problem addressed by Phases~1--2 of \method.

\paragraph{Problem B: cell synthesis.}
A CMOS standard cell implementing $f(\mathbf{x})$ consists of two
complementary networks realized with different transistor types.

\begin{definition}[PDN and PUN]
\label{def:pdnpun}
The \emph{pull-down network} (PDN) is an NMOS transistor network connected
between $\mathrm{GND}$ and the output $\mathrm{ZN}$; the \emph{pull-up
network} (PUN) is a PMOS transistor network connected between $\mathrm{VDD}$
and $\mathrm{ZN}$.
The cell is correct iff the PDN pulls $\mathrm{ZN}$ low whenever $f = 0$ and
the PUN pulls $\mathrm{ZN}$ high whenever $f = 1$.
\end{definition}

Problem~B is not a new problem: it decomposes into two instances of
Problem~A.

\begin{proposition}[Dual Switching Network Equivalence]
\label{prop:dual}
Let $\Pon$ and $\Poff$ denote the on- and off-patterns of the cell function
$f$.
A CMOS cell correctly implements $f(\mathbf{x})$ if and only if its PDN
correctly implements $\lnot f(\mathbf{x})$, with on-patterns $\Poff$, and its
PUN correctly implements $f(\lnot\mathbf{x})$, with on-patterns
$\{\lnot\pi : \pi \in \Pon\}$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
The proof, together with a worked AND2 decomposition, is given in the
supplementary material (App.~A--B).
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual} is what allows a \emph{single} policy to serve the
whole framework: the PDN and PUN are two instances of Problem~A that share the
same Boolean structure and differ only in their on/off pattern sets, so a
policy conditioned on those sets is agnostic to transistor type.
This is the foundation for the joint PDN+PUN training of Phase~3
(Section~\ref{sec:physical}).

\subsection{Physical Evaluation of a Placed Cell}
\label{sec:pre_phys}

A solved instance of Problem~B is not yet a layout.
Its transistors must be placed into two rows, and because adjacent devices
share a diffusion region only when their abutting terminals carry the same
net, the \emph{ordering} within each row, and not only the device count,
determines the area the cell occupies.
Quality is therefore measured on the placed cell by five metrics: cell width
in contacted poly pitch (CPP) units, gate mismatches between the P and N rows,
wire length, routing density, and diffusion cuts. Definitions of the five metrics are given in App.~C.
We use ASTRAN~\cite{astran}, a standard-cell layout tool, to place each
synthesized PDN+PUN pair and to report these quantities; their weighted sum
$\Phi$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:phi}) is the objective Phase~3 of \method{} minimizes
directly as the RL reward (Section~\ref{sec:physical}).
